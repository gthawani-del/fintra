# Woh Lamhe — director execution brief

Act as an experienced music-video director, editor and typography designer. Deliver a complete 133.24-second romantic memory film using the original song, the established fictional couple, existing beach images and clip, and purposeful new B-roll. Do not describe a technical render as proof of creative quality.

## Direction: What the tide remembers
The couple represents remembered intimacy. The shoreline represents time continuing without them. Build a progression from closeness, through distance, to acceptance. Return to the same ocean motif with a changed emotional meaning. Avoid literal illustration of every lyric and repetitive couple/photo loops.

## Picture and edit
Use natural sunset photography with visible skin and fabric texture, warm light from screen left and the rocky headland to the right. Preserve character faces, clothes and geography. Mix wide geography, medium relationship frames and tactile details. Use four new continuous B-roll sources: foam crossing sand; two footprint trails being erased; pale floral fabric moving in a coastal breeze, without a new face; empty shoreline after the couple is gone. Give each insert narrative purpose. Never present a crop as newly filmed motion.

Use full-frame 9:16 compositions. Cut on musical phrases, motion matches and emotional changes. Use a brief dissolve only for a deliberate change of memory/time. No iris, slats, split faces, page turns, generic wave wipes, flashing overlays or unmotivated camera moves. Quiet passages need sustained observation; the chorus needs emotional escalation, not arbitrary faster cutting.

## Complete lyric treatment
Recover every performed line from the actual recording and the existing draft. Treat the draft as unverified; use transcription as evidence, not authority. Resolve disagreements before final burned-in delivery. Do not omit hard passages and call the result complete. Do not invent inaudible words. Maintain an explicit unresolved-lines ledger.

Display complete phrases in sentence case, generally two lines at most, with natural line breaks and enough reading time. Use a refined serif for lyrical passages and a restrained sans-serif only for secondary text. No condensed uppercase poster font, handwriting, marker lettering or karaoke bounce. Keep the complete phrase visible while applying subtle masked entry, tracking settlement and selective emphasis to a meaningful word. Never shrink preceding words into an unreadable history line. Position text in planned negative space away from faces and hands; verify all glyphs, wrapping, mobile readability and frame edges.

## Production and acceptance
Preserve the original song at offset zero. Use official Lemo for deterministic composition and FFmpeg for encoding/muxing. Keep all code on feature/lemo-motion-studio under lemo-motion-studio/. Keep private source media outside Git history.

Before paid generation, obtain exact quotes and a bounded spending authorization; no silent retries. Inspect each generated clip for continuity, realistic water/fabric motion, anatomy, temporal defects and unwanted text. Replace only a failed shot. Render a short representative sequence with complete phrases and actual B-roll before the final film. Inspect the encoded result, not only source images. Deliver a verified H.264/AAC MP4 and record remaining limitations honestly.
