# रामायण — वचन से विजय तक
## Hindi screenplay and production plan • Draft 1

**Proposed runtime:** 11:00. **Format:** 1920×1080, 30fps. **Project:** gthawani-del/fintra, feature/lemo-motion-studio. **Status:** reviewable screenplay and production plan; full film, songs and new narration have not been generated.

An abridged retelling of the main journey through the return and coronation. This is not the entire seven-book epic. Childhood episodes are compressed into the introduction; Uttara Kanda, including Sita's later exile and Lava–Kusha, is outside this film. The existing 45-second proof remains a visual test, not a complete narrative sequence.

The emotional spine: a promise separates a family; loyalty and courage bring them through exile, loss and war. Sita has agency and resolve, Bharata refuses personal gain, and Hanuman acts with both strength and judgment. Battle is consequential, not a celebratory weapons montage.

All narration and song lyrics below are newly written adaptation text, not quotations from scripture or existing songs. Narrative order is informed by the Valmiki Ramayana; modern paintings, TV character designs and copyrighted recordings are not templates.

## 3. Two original song passages

These are lyric drafts and arrangement briefs, not generated recordings. The narrator does not suddenly become a singer. Use an off-screen singing voice; no lip-sync requirement for these montages.

### Song 1: साथ चले तो राह बने • 02:00–02:40 • 40 seconds

**Purpose:** turn departure into shared resolve, preserving sadness without stopping the story. Gentle female lead, intimate delivery, natural Hindi pronunciation; no imitation of a named singer.

**Original lyric:**

घर की देहरी पीछे छूटी,
आँखों में वह आँगन है।
पगडंडी अनजानी सही,
संग तुम्हारा जीवन है।

साथ चले तो राह बने,
रात ढले तो भोर मिले।
धूप मिले या छाँव मिले,
हाथ से अपना हाथ मिले।

**Arrangement:** 72 BPM, 12 bars of 4/4 = 40s. Bars 1–4 verse, 5–8 refrain, 9–10 repeat “साथ चले तो राह बने”, 11–12 instrumental answer. Plucked-string motif, quiet hand percussion, airy flute response. No long introduction; vocal enters in bar 1. Arrangement is a production proposal, not a claim of an authentic classical raga.

### Song 2: पार जाना है • 05:59–06:35 • 36 seconds

**Purpose:** convert remembered strength into action. Warm male lead or small ensemble, clear consonants, energetic but not shouted; no celebrity imitation.

**Original lyric:**

सागर गहरा, राह नहीं,
मन में फिर भी हार नहीं।
एक संदेशा साथ लिए,
पार जाना है।

पवन चले तो संग चलें,
लहर उठे तो ऊपर उठें।
आशा की यह डोर लिए,
लौट के आना है।

**Arrangement:** 100 BPM, 15 bars of 4/4 = 36s. Bars 1–4 first stanza, 5–8 second stanza, 9–12 alternate “पार जाना है / लौट के आना है”, 13–15 instrumental landing. Frame drum, low drum accents, plucked strings and one bright melodic response. Start with the leap, not an unrelated musical intro.

### What code does, and what it does not

Code can sequence MIDI notes, percussion, chord changes, instrument samples, transitions, dynamics and mixing. It can align existing sung audio, duck the score under narration and export stems. Convincing expressive singing is a separate performance-generation task. Ordinary TTS is not the singing solution.

Preferred path: a code-composed score plus two externally generated or recorded vocal passages, imported as WAV files. Suno is a possible supplier, not a booked generation. No paid song service, model, credit spend or voice-cloning workflow is authorized by this plan. If avoiding external singing entirely, the same two slots become instrumental sequences; remove lyric captions rather than pretending the melody is being sung.

## 4. Character and visual continuity

| Character | Persistent identity | Changes across the film |
|---|---|---|
| Rama | Same face, hair shape, physique, bow and restrained manner across media | Court dress → forest clothing → coronation dress; avoid crown in exile |
| Sita | Same face, hair, jewellery motifs; active gestures and independent gaze | Court refinement → practical forest clothing → austere captivity; no glamour lighting in distress |
| Lakshmana | Distinct face and silhouette from Rama, alert posture | Travel gear and protective stance; injury shown through posture and aftermath |
| Bharata | Distinct clothing and softer silhouette; not a duplicate Rama | Prince → austere stewardship → reunion |
| Hanuman | Approved ivory-paper design, red cloth and gold detailing | Flight, landing, listening, carrying and kneeling need different poses |
| Kaikeyi | Crimson/gold puppet palette | Resolve, hesitation and aftermath through shoulders, hands and head angles |
| Dasharatha | Aged face, crown, teal/gold puppet identity | Authority → shock → grief; do not rely only on lowering brightness |
| Ravana | Distinct imposing profile and consistent crown system | Court authority → refusal → battle; ten-head depiction, if used, must be consistent and planned |

Keep three styles. Oil painting handles place and intimate emotion. Shadow theatre handles dialogue, conflict and moral decisions. Layered paper handles travel, scale and collective action. Do not switch style every shot merely to demonstrate effects.

The current shadow test is functional but visually simpler than the oil and paper scenes. Before extending it, refine faces, costume silhouettes, hand poses and palace depth in a short test. Do not stretch the existing wide shot to fill 55 seconds.

## 5. Motion and typography rules

- Each shot must carry a new action, reaction or piece of information. Average target is about 9 seconds, with 2–4-second inserts and longer emotional holds.
- Every major action gets anticipation, action and follow-through. For portraits: eye-line, hand action and body weight matter more than extra zoom.
- Camera and character movement remain separate. Use cut-on-action, foreground occlusion and sound bridges; dissolves only for genuine changes of time or place.
- Original approved images and rigs may be reused as assets. Finished footage is reused only where its framing and action fit. No repeating a ten-second flight to create a minute.
- Narration: Noto Sans Devanagari Bold700, complete short phrases, ivory with a gold active word, immediate dark backing. Keep the current approved readability.
- Songs: two-line lyric groups, bold, highlight by sung phrase after alignment. Do not guess syllable timing from the text or force narration-style word ticks onto sustained notes.
- Caption placement must be checked against hands, faces and important props. Put the ring and sandals above the caption zone.
- Do not bake words into generated artwork. Render all text deterministically and retain editable timing data and SRT.

## 6. Audio plan

Retain the approved narrator identity only after recovering the original voice configuration and confirming a new sample matches it. The existing recording proves the voice sound, not that its complete generation settings are currently known. New narration is needed for the full script.

Use three recurring original musical ideas: a measured promise motif; a sparse separation motif; a rising resolve motif. Reprise the first at the end with fuller instrumentation. Avoid continuous dense music. Leave space after Dasharatha's response, Jatayu's death, the ring recognition and Ravana's fall.

Foley belongs to the medium: cloth and footsteps for painted scenes, quiet hide/rod taps for shadow theatre, paper movement and restrained wind for paper scenes. Keep effects quieter than speech. Deliver separate narration, score, song and effects stems. Measure loudness after the full mix; do not blindly normalize each shot independently.

## 7. Production order and acceptance gates

1. Review this screenplay, the ending scope and the two song treatments. This is the current deliverable.
2. Recover narrator settings; create one new voice check. Lock narration language/pronunciation for Kaikeyi, Jatayu, Kishkindha, Vibhishana and other names before recording all chapters.
3. Build character sheets and environment plates. Prove Sita, Ravana and the improved shadow style early; they are the main missing visual foundations.
4. Produce chapter narration and measure durations. Revise shot durations to the performance. Target 11:00; allow 10–12 minutes rather than artificial pauses or rushed speech.
5. Compose the instrumental score in code. Obtain the two singing passages only through the agreed provider or supplied recordings; otherwise make instrumental replacements.
6. Render a rough full-length animatic with all narration and temporary score. Verify story comprehension, omissions and pace before final-detail renders.
7. Finish in three batches: chapters 1–4, 5–8 and 9–12. Inspect actual frames and action strips; fix anatomy, puppet joints, occlusion and captions before assembly.
8. Assemble at a single frame rate/timebase. Check video and audio both reach the intended ending, every word matches the correct character/event, transitions retain speech, and the final title holds long enough.
9. Final review: complete playback with sound plus frame checks, decode, black/freeze tests, clipping and loudness checks. Record what was actually reviewed; do not label sampled-frame QA as full audiovisual review.

**Final outputs after production:** film MP4, poster, Hindi SRT, caption timing data, audio stems, source and one-command build scripts, credits and QA record. Source belongs on the requested fintra branch; media deliverables are separate.
