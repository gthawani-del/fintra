# Beach Memory Film: custom Lemo visual style (OpenAI agent compatible)

**Intent:** The original Woh Lamhe audio becomes a visually engaging love story on an Indian-coast-inspired beach. Two recurring **fictional** adult characters stay identifiable across the film. Warm memories shift into cool present-day isolation, and the final chorus briefly reunites them as a memory.

## Film grammar
- **Characters first.** Faces and body language must be legible. Do not dim the characters into silhouettes for most of the film. Use consistent character assets, wardrobe and direction of movement.
- **Cinematic moving pictures.** Prefer short actual moving shots for walking, turning, holding hands, glancing, separation, hair movement, and shore surf. Still-photo camera moves are acceptable only for intentional memory tableaux and establishing inserts.
- **Shot variety.** Wide beach establishing, tracking two-shot, profile close-up, over-shoulder, footprints insert, reflection cut, top-down wave, lone subject, reveal at chorus.
- **Progression.** First 30s: lovers together. 30–51s: emotional distance emerges. 51–71s: the chorus visibly transforms footage. 71–98s: alone in the same geography. Guitar: waves, memory fragments and rhythm changes. Final chorus: memory/absence contrast.
- **Palette.** Memories: honey, peach and warm sandstone; present: slate blue and desaturated sand. Protect natural skin tones and visible separation between subjects/background.
- **Typography.** Lyrics integrated into negative space, not a static bottom caption. Build at least three *genuinely different* motion behaviors: beat-timed masked word reveals, split/perspective type, chorus-scale emphasis with words appearing on vocal hits; optional foamy-wave letter erasure. Text must stay readable at mobile size.
- **Transitions.** Motivated by water, shadows, camera motion or a character's position. Avoid generic fades at every scene boundary.
- **Sync.** Every cue needs aligned start/end; instrumental sections show no invented lyric. Code-switched Hindi/English segments require review.

## Reuse and licensing
The couple should use the user's approved generated images as identity references or properly licensed acting footage. Do **not** silently replace them with unrelated stock couples. If source assets are unavailable to the cloud runner, stop before rendering and report the missing asset. Avoid unsanctioned third-party likenesses.

## Proof criteria before expensive full render
1. Render three 5-second excerpts (verse/chorus/instrumental) in the real Lemo browser renderer.
2. A contact sheet must show visible human subjects, ocean and legible lyric motion.
3. Compare frames 0.20s apart to confirm movement, not a static still with a fading caption.
4. Review exact lyric starts/ends against the attached audio.
5. Only then export the complete 133.272s film.
